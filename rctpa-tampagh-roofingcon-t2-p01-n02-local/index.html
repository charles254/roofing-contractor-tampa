Understanding the Florida 25% Roof Rule in Tampa & Hillsborough County

Florida Building Code Section 706.1.1 (Existing Building) establishes the renowned 25% Roof Replacement Rule, a critical regulation governing whether a damaged roof can be locally patched or must undergo full replacement. For homeowners and commercial property managers in Tampa, understanding how municipal building inspectors apply this rule is paramount when assessing storm claims and budgeting capital repairs.

Statutory Framework: Florida Statute 553.844 & FBC Chapter 7

The rule stipulates that if more than 25 percent of a roof system or roof section is repaired, replaced, or recovered within a single 12-month period, the entire roof section must be reconstructed to conform to the requirements of the current Florida Building Code. Recent legislative amendments provide exemptions only for roofs built or replaced under the 2007 FBC or later that meet specific structural integrity standards.

Frequently asked questions

What defines a "roof section" under the Florida 25% rule?

A roof section is defined as a contiguous roof plane or distinct individual roofing surface terminated by ridges, valleys, eaves, or parapet walls that function as an independent drainage area.

Will my insurance carrier pay for a full replacement under the 25% rule?

If your policy includes Ordinance or Law coverage (standard or endorsed on most Florida policies), the carrier is required to pay the additional costs to bring the entire roof into compliance with current building codes when more than 25% is damaged.
