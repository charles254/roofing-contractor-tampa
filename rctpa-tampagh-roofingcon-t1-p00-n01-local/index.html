Critical Indicators of Storm Damage & Concealed Roof Deck Compromise

Severe hurricane winds often inflict structural damage that remains invisible from ground level. Shingle tabs can crease and break their thermal sealant strip, while concrete and clay tiles can chatter against one another, cracking water barriers beneath. Property owners should recognize early indicators of structural distress before moisture compromises interior drywall, insulation, and electrical systems.

  • Missing, creased, or lifted shingle tabs along windward eaves, rakes, and vulnerable roof hips
  • Chipped, cracked, or sliding Spanish clay and concrete barrel tiles exposing unsealed underlayment
  • Excessive granule accumulation in gutters and downspouts signaling accelerated shingle aging and UV breakdown
  • Water stains, peeling ceiling paint, or damp attic insulation following heavy wind-driven rain events

What is the Florida 25 Percent Roof Rule and how does it affect roof replacements in Tampa?

Under the Florida Building Code and Florida Statute 553.844, if more than 25 percent of a total roofing section is repaired, replaced, or damaged within a 12-month period, the entire roofing section must be brought into compliance with current building codes, typically necessitating a full roof replacement.

How much can a Wind Mitigation Inspection save on Tampa property insurance?

A certified Wind Mitigation Inspection (Form OIR-B1-1802) documenting secondary water barriers, hurricane clips or wraps, and ring-shank roof deck nailing can reduce annual homeowner insurance premiums by 15% to over 40% across Florida.
