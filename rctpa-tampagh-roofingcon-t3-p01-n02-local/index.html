Tampa Secondary Water Barrier (SWR) & Code Roof Deck Fastening

The true line of defense against catastrophic hurricane water damage is not the outer shingle or tile, but the underlying roof deck fastening and secondary water barrier. Under the Florida Building Code, every roof tear-off in Hillsborough County requires structural deck re-nailing and high-performance secondary water resistance (SWR) membranes before surface materials are applied.

8d Ring-Shank Re-Nailing: Doubling Roof Sheathing Holding Power

Historic homes in Tampa often have roof decking secured with smooth-shank staples or 6d nails spaced 12 inches apart. During hurricane wind suction, entire plywood sheets can detach. We re-nail all decking with 8d ring-shank nails spaced at 6 inches along panel edges, dramatically increasing uplift resistance.

Self-Adhering Polymer-Modified Bitumen SWR Underlayment

Traditional 15-lb or 30-lb felt paper tears easily in 50 mph winds and absorbs water. We apply self-adhering modified bitumen membranes directly to clean roof decking, forming an impermeable monolithic barrier that seals tightly around fastener shanks.

  • Direct-to-deck adhesion preventing water travel between membrane and plywood
  • High-temperature formulations engineered to withstand summer attic heat up to 250°F
  • UV-resistant exposure ratings allowing up to 180 days of dry-in weather protection
  • Full compliance with Florida Building Code Form OIR-B1-1802 SWR requirements
